# Storyscope research boundary

Storyscope adapts findings from Russell et al., “StoryScope: Investigating
idiosyncrasies in AI fiction,” arXiv:2604.03136.

The study used a controlled fiction corpus: 10,272 human-written stories and
stories generated by five language models from the same prompts, yielding 61,608
stories and 304 narrative features across ten dimensions: Agent, Social Network,
Event, Plot, Structure, Setting, Time, Revelation, Perspective, and Style.

The reported classifier results were 93.2% macro-F1 for human-versus-AI detection
without the style dimension and 68.4% macro-F1 for six-way source attribution.
The study reported group-level tendencies such as explicit themes, tidy single-
track causality, embodied explanation, and resolution through internal
understanding in generated stories, alongside more structural variety, social
world, intertextual specificity, temporal complexity, subplots, morally mixed
protagonists, and reader inference in the human corpus.

These are hypotheses for editorial questioning, not universal laws. The corpus is
fiction-specific, the human stories come from a Books3-derived research dataset,
and the paper does not establish that a blog, technical chapter, or individual
sentence is human- or AI-written. Do not invert every observed tendency. Do not
use the reported percentages as a quality target or publication gate.
